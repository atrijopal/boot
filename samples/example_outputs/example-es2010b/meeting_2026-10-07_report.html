The meeting reviewed remote‑control product features, component choices, power options, and internal design.

Summary

  • The team discussed defining the remote's functionality by the devices it controls, such as DVD players or TiVo, to broaden consumer appeal.
  • Research was cited indicating consumers are willing to pay more for speech‑recognition capability, positioning it as a high‑class feature.
  • A beeping feature was proposed to help users locate a lost remote, mirroring rival products.
  • It was noted that a smart chip is required for multifunctionality, or an additional chip would be needed for extra functions.
  • The smart chip was described as a programmed component that can perform a fair range of functions.
  • Cost concerns about an additional chip were dismissed, with the belief bulk purchasing from a newer supplier would keep production costs within budget.
  • Batteries were selected as the power source because they are readily available and standard for remotes, with the number depending on size.
  • Typical remotes use two AA or four AAA batteries, sometimes three, as a reference point for the design.
  • A large on/off button was recommended to accommodate an older target demographic.
  • The internal schematic includes a switch between the power source and the rest of the device, and an infrared bulb for communication with various devices.
  • Adding a second infrared bulb was suggested to indicate functionality without requiring users to stare at the primary bulb when the battery is low.
